Operating-model design / Knowledge architecture / Leadership collaboration

Turning company strategy into coordinated action

Teams were busy, but their effort was not consistently connected to company goals. I led the design of a shared operating model with senior leadership, then built the system that made priorities, ownership and knowledge visible across teams, giving strategy a practical route into delivery.

02 / The challenge

Busy teams, disconnected priorities

Strategic context often stayed within leadership conversations instead of reaching the teams responsible for delivery. Information lived in private folders, documents, Slack conversations and people’s memories, with little visibility beyond each person’s own work.

Teams duplicated effort or progressed work that later became redundant because it was not aligned with wider goals. Priorities changed without a clear, shared explanation, interrupting projects and making it difficult for people to understand the value of their contribution.

The problem was a missing connection between strategy and execution. Teams needed a common basis for deciding what mattered, coordinating their effort and using shared knowledge to build on work already done.

03 / The approach

Start with how the company works

I started by helping senior leadership clarify company goals and prioritise the work that would support them. We connected those priorities to the Value Engines through which the company created value, then translated them into team initiatives and delivery. The operating model came before the workspace.

I brought postgraduate study in Global Business Leadership and experience running my own business to this work. Drawing on Lean, OKRs, EOS and Value Engines, I helped leadership connect strategic ambition to operating decisions: what to prioritise, who should own it and how teams could deliver it together.

Company goals → Value Engines → teams → key initiatives → projects → SOPs → daily tasks

Three annual company goals connected to Growth, Fulfilment and Innovation Value Engines. The hierarchy gave leadership and teams a way to assess priorities at each level, explain the purpose of a project and check whether daily work supported the broader direction.

04 / System architecture

One set of information, different views of the work

I built the Notion system around connected databases, making goals, initiatives, projects, people, knowledge and SOPs visible in relation to one another. Team-specific views showed relevant work while preserving its connection to wider company priorities.

Ownership, dates, status and dependencies gave teams a shared view of what was underway and where their input was needed. Connecting knowledge to delivery made existing work and guidance easier to find and reuse.

Alignment could also mean a small change to existing work. Linking a marketing asset to a wider project and a clear call to action made it part of a connected funnel, with a purpose beyond producing another standalone deliverable.

05 / Adoption and ownership

Making it usable without making myself indispensable

Adoption depended on a shared understanding of priorities and responsibilities. I worked with leadership to align goals and initiatives, explain how priorities should cascade to teams, and establish ownership for review and upkeep. Guidance helped teams use and maintain the system themselves.

The OS was adopted across 10 teams, supporting 30–33 employees. Rollout included around ten leaders trained and approximately five leadership workshops and training sessions.

Workshops + individual training

Leadership enablement

Through workshops and individual training, I helped leaders connect company goals to team initiatives, assess priorities and explain the purpose of the work beneath them. This gave leadership a shared framework for directing effort and communicating decisions across teams.

Review cadence + ownership rules

Governance built in

I established responsibilities for reviewing and maintaining priorities, projects and shared knowledge. Clear ownership and review routines gave teams a way to keep the system useful as priorities changed, with accountability built into everyday delivery.

Onboarding + reusable guidance

Self-service by design

I created onboarding guidance and reusable templates so teams could navigate the system, document processes and maintain their own work. This distributed responsibility for upkeep across the organisation, helping the operating model remain usable without depending on its original builder.

How the value was estimated

The model uses a representative workforce of 32 within the reported 30–33 employees. Recovering 1.5–3 hours per person each week equates to 48–96 hours weekly, or 2,496–4,992 hours on a 52-week annualised basis. The same weekly saving is assumed across every salary band.
