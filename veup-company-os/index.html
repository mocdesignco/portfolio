Operating-model design / Knowledge architecture / Leadership collaboration

Giving company priorities somewhere to live

VeUP’s work was spread across tools, documents and individual knowledge. I worked with the CEO and senior leadership to design a shared operating model, then built the Notion system that connected it to everyday work.

02 / The challenge

The context was there, but finding it took work

Information lived in private folders, presentations, documents, Slack conversations and people’s memories. Employees often had to reconstruct why something mattered before they could act on it. Leadership priorities were difficult to follow through to active projects.

A shared workspace needed to make those relationships visible. Simply giving the documents a tidier home wouldn’t resolve how priorities, ownership and delivery fitted together.

Private foldersPresentationsDocumentsSlack conversationsIndividual knowledge

03 / The approach

Start with how the company works

I designed the operating model before building the workspace. Working with the CEO and senior leadership, I defined a structure linking company goals to the teams and work responsible for delivering them.

I drew on business-leadership study, experience running my own company and principles from Lean, OKRs, EOS and Value Engines, combining the elements that suited VeUP.

Company goals → Value Engines → teams → key initiatives → projects → SOPs → daily tasks

Three annual company goals connected to Growth, Fulfilment and Innovation Value Engines, then to team priorities, projects and owners. That structure gave the workspace its purpose.

04 / System architecture

One set of information, different views of the work

I built the Notion system around connected databases. A team could see the information relevant to its work while retaining the links to wider goals and initiatives.

The architecture brought together people, priorities, projects, knowledge and standard operating procedures, with ownership, dates, status and reusable templates. Those connections made it possible to follow a priority through to the work beneath it.

05 / Adoption and ownership

Making it usable without making myself indispensable

The system needed people who could maintain it. I worked with leadership to align goals and initiatives, establish responsibilities for review and upkeep, and create guidance teams could use themselves.

The rollout included approximately ten team hubs, around ten leaders trained and approximately five leadership workshops and training sessions.

I developed the system for a company of roughly 34 employees plus contractors.

06 / What changed

What changed

Delivered: A shared structure connecting company priorities with work, ownership and knowledge, supported by governance and training.

Context, ownership and status became easier to inspect within the workspace. Leadership could see more clearly how active work related to priorities, while teams had a shared structure for managing their work.

07 / What it demonstrates

What this project demonstrates

My ability to connect company strategy to everyday work through shared visibility, clearer priorities and better communication, helping teams collaborate, avoid duplicated effort and work towards shared goals.
