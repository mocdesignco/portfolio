Customer insight / Brand positioning / Digital experience

A distinctive brand that left room for the client

Ashen & Cloud wanted fewer, better-fit projects. I developed the research, positioning and identity around a particular balance: enough presence to communicate expertise, enough restraint to let each client’s taste lead.

02 / Ashen & Cloud

Defining what premium meant for this business

The founder wanted to work with fewer clients on larger, more personalised projects. Looking more luxurious was only useful if it helped the right people recognise the service and understand its value.

I researched how premium interior studios presented themselves through their identities, language, websites and client experience. I then compared those approaches with what Ashen & Cloud’s intended customer valued: quality, trust and expertise, alongside a home that felt unmistakably her own.

03 / Ashen & Cloud

Recognition without a prescribed aesthetic

A recognisable house style can make an interior studio memorable. It can also leave a prospective client wondering how much room there is for their own taste.

That was the tension I wanted the positioning to resolve. Ashen & Cloud could have a distinctive identity while allowing the interiors themselves to vary. The studio’s expertise would come through in how it interpreted a client’s needs, rather than in a fixed look applied to every project.

The resulting principles were personal rather than prescriptive, expert without being intimidating, and distinctive without dominating the work.

04 / Ashen & Cloud

Choosing the visual direction

I developed two routes exploring different expressions of premium. I assessed them against the positioning and the way the customer needed to experience the studio.

The selected direction used stronger typography within a restrained visual system. It gave the brand recognition while leaving space for different interiors to be the focus.

I carried that approach through the wordmark and monogram, colour, typography, tone of voice and editorial layouts, as well as proposals, project presentations and business materials.

Route 01

Classic aspiration.

Strategic rationale

Serif typography, deep charcoal, chocolate and burgundy. A warmer, more overtly affluent world built around timeless elegance and established luxury codes.

Selected direction

Creative versatility.

Strategic rationale

A more contemporary, restrained system designed as a blank canvas for the interiors. Strong typography created recognition while a quieter palette gave different projects room to breathe.

05 / Ashen & Cloud

Making the experience match the promise

I designed and built a custom Webflow website and developed client documentation around the same positioning.

For a service that asks people to invest substantially in their homes, trust starts well before the design work. The website and proposal needed to communicate the care and clarity clients could expect from the studio. Content and document guidance gave the founder a way to sustain that experience beyond launch.

06 / Ashen & Cloud

What changed

Delivered: A clearer premium position, expressed through the identity, website and client-facing materials.

The founder confirmed more manageable enquiry volumes and better alignment with the intended client profile and budgets. For a business deliberately seeking fewer clients, that quality mattered more than a busier inbox.

Instagram audience growth was approximately 34% in the six months following the rebrand.

Reported business effects: Higher-quality inbound leads and a shorter sales process following the rebrand.

“The result was much more than a visual transformation. I had a brand that reflected the quality of my work, a clearer way to communicate its value and greater confidence in how I positioned the business.”

Susan McGowan
Founder & Creative Director
