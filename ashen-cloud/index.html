Customer insight / Brand positioning / Digital experience

A distinctive brand that left room for the client

Ashen & Cloud wanted fewer, better-fit projects. I developed the research, positioning and identity around a particular balance: enough presence to communicate expertise, enough restraint to let each client’s taste lead.

02 / Customer and market insight

Defining what premium meant for this business

The founder wanted to work with fewer clients on larger, more personalised projects. Looking more luxurious was only useful if it helped the right people recognise the service and understand its value.

I carried out a structured competitor analysis, comparing four premium interior studios across their positioning, distinctive strengths, visual identities, tone of voice, websites and client experience. I mapped the wider market to identify where Ashen & Cloud could stand apart: a recognisable brand with a personal service, without imposing a fixed aesthetic on every home.

I developed a detailed customer persona covering buying behaviour, values, motivations, time pressures and the trust needed to commission a substantial home project. I also considered audience awareness and how clients discover, evaluate and choose a studio.

The central insight was that this customer needed more than a luxurious look. She needed confidence in the studio’s expertise, a clear and reassuring experience, and room for her own taste. The opportunity was to make Ashen & Cloud distinctive without making its clients’ homes look alike.

03 / Brand strategy and positioning

Recognition without a prescribed aesthetic

In response, I positioned Ashen & Cloud around tailored interior design: expertise that interprets each client’s needs and creates a home that feels distinctly theirs. The brand needed to communicate premium quality and a personal experience, rather than promise a signature look applied to every project.

I translated that position into the brand story, mission, values, personality and tone of voice. Marketing recommendations focused on nurturing an already interested audience through design stories, insights into the process and personalised communication, helping prospective clients understand the service and build confidence in it.

The guiding principles were personal rather than prescriptive, expert without being intimidating, and distinctive without dominating the work. These gave the visual development a clear brief: create recognition for the studio while leaving space for the individuality of its clients and interiors.

04 / Visual direction

Choosing the visual direction

With that strategy established, I developed two visual routes showing different ways to express premium quality, personal service and creative expertise. Both responded to the same positioning: Route 1 through classic luxury cues, Route 2 through a more contemporary, versatile identity.

I presented both routes to give the founder a clear choice and room to shape how the strategy would look. She selected Route 2, Creative versatility. Stronger typography within a restrained system gave the brand recognition while leaving different interiors room to lead.

I then carried the selected direction through the wordmark and monogram, colour, typography and editorial layouts, as well as proposals, project presentations and business materials, guided by the agreed positioning and tone of voice.

Route 01

Classic aspiration.

Strategic rationale

Serif typography, deep charcoal, chocolate and burgundy. A warmer, more overtly affluent world built around timeless elegance and established luxury codes.

Selected direction

Creative versatility.

Strategic rationale

A more contemporary, restrained system designed as a blank canvas for the interiors. Strong typography created recognition while a quieter palette gave different projects room to breathe.

05 / Digital and client experience

Making the experience match the promise

I designed and built a custom Webflow website and developed client documentation around the same positioning.

For a service that asks people to invest substantially in their homes, trust starts well before the design work. The website and proposal needed to communicate the care and clarity clients could expect from the studio. Content and document guidance gave the founder a way to sustain that experience beyond launch.

06 / What changed

What changed

Delivered: A clearer premium position, expressed through the identity, website and client-facing materials.

The founder confirmed more manageable enquiry volumes and better alignment with the intended client profile and budgets. For a business deliberately seeking fewer clients, that quality mattered more than a busier inbox.

Instagram audience growth was approximately 34% in the six months following the rebrand.

Reported business effects: Higher-quality inbound leads and a shorter sales process following the rebrand.

“The result was much more than a visual transformation. I had a brand that reflected the quality of my work, a clearer way to communicate its value and greater confidence in how I positioned the business.”

Susan McGowan
Founder & Creative Director
