Ashen & Cloud

Designing a luxury brand around the client's taste, not the designer's. Ashen & Cloud needed a distinctive position in a crowded premium interiors market. I used competitor and customer research to find the opportunity, then carried it through positioning, voice, identity, website and client experience.

ClientAshen & Cloud
RoleBrand strategist & designer
ScopeStrategy → identity → digital
QuestionCan insight change the work?
01 / The evidence

Start with the market. Then the customer.

The brief was not simply to make Ashen & Cloud look more luxurious.

I looked at how premium interior studios positioned themselves across identity, copy, digital experience and client experience, then compared that with what Ashen & Cloud's target customer actually valued.

Market lens

Distinctive can become restrictive.

Competitor research showed that a recognisable house aesthetic can build recall, but can also make the designer's taste feel like the product. That created space for a premium brand with stronger recognition and broader aesthetic versatility.
Customer lens

Expertise without surrendering individuality.

The target customer was affluent and design-conscious, but also time-poor and considered in how she bought. She wanted quality, trust and expertise, while still wanting the finished home to feel unmistakably her own.
02 / The insight

In a category built around taste, the opportunity wasn't to impose more of it.

MarketDistinctive designer aesthetics create recognition.
CustomerWants expertise, reassurance and a home that still feels uniquely theirs.
OpportunityMake the service and point of view distinctive, without prescribing the client's aesthetic.
03 / The strategy

Turn the insight into a choice.

The positioning centred Ashen & Cloud on tailored, highly personalised interior design.

The strategy needed to feel premium without becoming distant, distinctive without overpowering the work, and confident enough to lead while leaving room for the client's own point of view.

Personal, not prescriptive.

The client's taste remains central. The designer brings expertise, interpretation and craft rather than a fixed aesthetic.

Expert, not intimidating.

Sophistication and exclusivity are balanced with warmth, clarity and approachability.

Distinctive, not dominant.

The Ashen & Cloud system creates recognition while allowing each interior project to carry its own personality.

04 / Creative direction

Test what the positioning could look like.

Two visual routes explored two different interpretations of premium.

The decision was not about which moodboard looked nicest. It was about which direction best supported the market position and the way the customer wanted to experience the brand.

Route 01

Classic aspiration.

Serif typography, deep charcoal, chocolate and burgundy. A warmer, more overtly affluent world built around timeless elegance and established luxury codes.

Selected direction

Creative versatility.

A more contemporary, restrained system designed as a blank canvas for the interiors. Strong typography created recognition while a quieter palette gave different projects room to breathe.

05 / Strategy into identity

The brand provides the frame. The work provides the personality.

The resulting identity could be recognisable without becoming another interior style the customer had to buy into. Bold typography and the AC shorthand created ownability; restrained layouts allowed project imagery to remain the hero. Hover over the work for the strategic rationale.

06 / The experience

Carry the strategy past the logo.

The same idea shaped how prospective clients discovered, evaluated and experienced the studio.

I translated the positioning into a custom Webflow website, project-led editorial presentation and branded client documentation. The system had to support a high-consideration service where trust, clarity and presentation matter long before a room is designed.

07 / A usable system

Make the strategy survive contact with the real world.

The work connected positioning, voice, identity and everyday brand use.

The tone of voice balanced sophistication and exclusivity with approachability and creativity. Guidance extended into content creation and business documents so the brand could stay coherent beyond the initial identity launch.

08 / The outcome

Insight that changed the work.

The result was more than a distinctive identity. It was a clearer position for Ashen & Cloud, built around what its customer valued: expertise without prescription, and a highly considered service that still felt personal.
