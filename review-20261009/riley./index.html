Brand strategy / Creative direction / Packaging

Building a brand for Riley’s next chapter

A period-care brand needed an identity that could speak to a more mature audience and feel at home in a wider range of settings. I led the strategy and creative direction, carrying the repositioning through packaging, brand applications and specialist production.

02 / Riley

A brand whose audience was changing

Riley’s pastel colours and playful identity made sense for its original direct-to-consumer audience. As the business developed, that expression needed to accommodate people making more considered choices about ingredients, product safety and sustainability, as well as buyers bringing period care into workplace wellbeing.

The identity needed to feel credible in those settings while retaining the warmth that matters in a personal-care brand. Maturity didn’t need to mean becoming clinical.

03 / Riley

Making Riley the considered choice

I focused the repositioning on customers who already understood the category and were questioning conventional period products. They needed a clear reason to choose Riley, with an identity that respected how thoughtfully they were making that decision.

04 / Riley

Carrying the thinking into the design

I developed the positioning, ideal-customer direction, tone of voice and messaging, then translated them into the visual system.

A deeper palette, more editorial typography and clearer hierarchy gave the brand greater confidence. Illustration and warmer human details kept it approachable. Those decisions carried through logo use, iconography, photography direction and brand applications.

Packaging was the most tangible test of the repositioning. It needed to feel considered on a bathroom shelf and credible in an office supply cupboard, with the same identity doing both jobs.

My remit also included merchandise and social applications, marketing direction, initial campaign concepts, slogans and suggested marketing plans. The system gave those different expressions a common foundation.

05 / Riley

Directing production before the product existed

The packaging was designed, but the physical packs hadn’t been printed. Waiting for them would have limited how we could show the new identity in context.

I directed a 3D artist and used AI-assisted image workflows to create product scenes and branded environments before print production was complete. The creative direction was already established; these tools helped make it visible.

Across 3D, illustration, social and packaging contributions, I set the shared framework and coordinated specialist work. That allowed different disciplines to develop the identity without pulling it in different directions.
